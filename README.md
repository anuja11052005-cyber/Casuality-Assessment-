# Causality Assessment

## Case 1: Amoxicillin – Skin Rash

**Patient:** 45-year-old female  
**Suspected Drug:** Amoxicillin  
**Adverse Drug Reaction:** Skin rash  
**Onset:** 3 days after starting amoxicillin  
**Dechallenge:** Rash improved/recovered after stopping amoxicillin  
**Rechallenge:** Not performed  

### Causality Assessment
The temporal relationship between starting amoxicillin and the appearance of the skin rash is appropriate. The reaction occurred 3 days after initiation of the drug and improved after discontinuation. No rechallenge was performed, and alternative causes cannot be completely excluded.

### Classification: PROBABLE

**Reason:** There is a reasonable time relationship and a positive dechallenge, but there is no rechallenge or definitive evidence establishing the drug as the only possible cause.

## Case 2: Metformin – Nausea and Diarrhea

**Patient:** Adult patient  
**Suspected Drug:** Metformin  
**Adverse Drug Reaction:** Nausea and diarrhea  
**Onset:** Shortly after starting metformin  
**Dechallenge:** Symptoms improved after discontinuing metformin  
**Rechallenge:** Not performed  

### Causality Assessment
The gastrointestinal symptoms developed after initiation of metformin and improved following withdrawal of the drug. Nausea and diarrhea are recognized adverse effects associated with metformin. However, other possible causes of gastrointestinal symptoms cannot be completely excluded.

### Classification: PROBABLE

**Reason:** The timing is compatible with the suspected drug, the symptoms improve after withdrawal, and the reaction is consistent with a known adverse effect of metformin. However, there is no rechallenge or definitive proof of causality.
